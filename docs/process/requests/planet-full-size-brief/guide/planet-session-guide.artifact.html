The route

a sessiona Fable reviewdonenextparked

Every session has the same shape

  1. It reads the progress page, the rules of work and its own part of the brief.
  2. It tells you the plan in plain words, and puts its decisions: three at most, all at once.
  3. It builds one change.
  4. It checks the formatter, the tests and the fingerprints.
  5. It makes the pictures the same six, before and after, at full size.
  6. You look and say yes or no.
  7. It writes the records then commits and pushes.

The sessions

Getting ready

Four sessions that change no planet. They put the paperwork, the engine and the pictures in place, and end with your own list of what good looks like.

Garcks-PC · Opus 5.5Documents onlyNext

What it is for

Puts Planet's paperwork in order, so every later session can start by reading a little and know what to do. It records your decisions of 27 September.

It also brings the last test runs home from the server, stops the Earth-size test planet and keeps its file, and puts Claude's tools of 27 September to you as one line to confirm.

You will be asked

  1. The documents as a whole: a shorter CLAUDE.md, a one-page PROGRESS.md, the old decisions file archived untouched and a fresh one begun, closed plans and research moved into folders.
  2. The three old worktrees removed. Their branches stay; this only frees disk.
  3. The habit's new wording: the best design wins, and the owner must be able to see it.

You should see

A docs folder with about a dozen files at its top, and a progress page that fits one screen.

Done when

A fresh session can start from CLAUDE.md, PROGRESS.md and the brief alone.

FS-6

Quieter coasts

Garcks-PC · Opus 5.5 · the test serverChanges the worldNext

The one change you will seeA trench only where one has started

What it is for

Not every closing border is a trench. Before, any two plates that closed at all made one, so half of all plate border was trench and the continents gained rock without end, which raised the sea. Now a border becomes a trench only once 150 km of plate has gone under it head-on.

Its first two sittings: the measuring, then this one change. The third sitting comes after FS-7 and has its own card below. Its planets were grown on the test server.

You will be asked

  1. What makes a closing border a trench. You chose the trench that must be started.
  2. The figure at which you are told the sea has risen too far. You chose 500 m.
  3. After the second review: keep the rule or undo it. You kept it.

You should see

At 5 billion years the sea's rise fell from 1,342 m to 86 m, land from 48% to 37%, and trench from half of all plate border to a quarter (Earth about a fifth). The walls on the coasts, the bowls and the specks have not moved yet.

Done when

You have seen the pictures and said yes. You did, on 29 September.

To start it

Done on 29 September. Its gallery is linked from Planet's docs/PROGRESS.md.

FS-7

A plate's life

Garcks-PC · Opus 5.5Changes the worldNext

The one change you will seeFewer small plates, and coasts left quiet

What it is for

Plates are born, join and die sensibly, and land is grouped as Earth's is. The large plates are already near Earth's dozen; the excess is 20 to 30 small ones. Most are old ocean floor that broke away from its own continent, each born with a trench on that coast. So this session opens on that rule, which also lines the coasts with trenches and walls.

It measures first. How two plates become one, and the smallest piece that may break away, follow in a later sitting if the pictures still ask for them.

You will be asked

  1. When old ocean floor may break away from its continent, and whether it is born with a trench.
  2. Whether heat gathering under a large continent at rest is what cracks it, in place of today's seeded chance. This is the first piece of the planet's inner heat.
  3. Anything the count of plates born and lost brings up.

You should see

The plates picture at 1, 2 and 3 billion years, before and after; the share of coast with a trench; and the largest landmass's share of the land.

Then Layer 3: climate. Rain that differs from place to place is what finishes the rivers and the erosion numbers, which is why they are not tuned before it.

What every session keeps to

  1. Full size is the judge. Nothing is kept on a small planet's evidence.
  2. Your eye is the gate. Numbers sit beside the pictures; they do not decide.
  3. One visible change a session. No second change until you have looked at the first.
  4. At most three decisions a session. All at its start, each with its options and a picture or number.
  5. Causes before numbers. A number is never turned to cancel another rule's side effect.
  6. Kilometres and years. No rule is written in cells or shares of the planet.
  7. A planet lives about 5 billion years. Rules are judged over that life.
  8. Tripwires tell; they do not choose. Land 25 to 40%, continent 32 to 45%.
  9. Many-planet runs only when a picture cannot answer. Three seeds and your eye are the usual test.
  10. Nothing is scripted. No schedules, and nothing steers a planet toward a figure.
  11. It stops and says so when one of the signs below appears.

Say stop when a session

  • puts more than three decisions to you, or a list to confirm
  • wants to judge a change on a small planet
  • proposes a second fix to cancel the side effects of the first
  • wants to loosen a test, or move a tripwire or a line of your look list
  • starts a second visible change before you have looked at the first
  • cannot show you the change in a picture

Each of these is a rule the session has agreed to. Saying stop costs nothing, and a Fable review is there when it happens twice running.
