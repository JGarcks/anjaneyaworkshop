1. **The sea never stops rising**, about 280 m a billion years, because the continents gain about 1 km³ of rock a year and nothing takes as much away. On the fixed scale the far inland ground rises with it, from about 500 m to about 1,900 m.
2. **The ground inland keeps up only by silting.** It stands about 200 m above the sea all life long, and the sea swings 200 to 330 m within 100 million years. One swing floods it. This is your "lakes come and go within 10 million years".
3. **Every continent is a bowl.** Seed 25660 at 1 billion years: land within 250 km of the open sea stands at 1,923 m, land beyond 3,000 km at 52 m. One river drains 134 million km², 68 % of all land, through one gap.
4. **The wall is on nearly every coast, quiet ones too.** Seen in the pictures, not measured: on seed 25660 at 1 billion years the north-east coast of the western lobe is quiet on `plates.png` and a wall on `heights.png`.
5. **Nothing makes height inland.** Half the land is born below about 160 m.

**Earth, for comparison (from memory; please check against `docs/research/` before a rule rests on them):** its continents make about 1 to 3 km³ of rock a year at their arcs and lose about as much at trenches, so they hardly grow; its sea has stayed within about 200 m of today's level for 500 million years; its land averages about 800 m above the sea.
